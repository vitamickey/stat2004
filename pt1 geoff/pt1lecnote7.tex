\chapter[Lec 7: Distribution of s squared]{Lec 7: Distribution of \(s^2\)}\label{ch:Lec 7:dist of s squared}

In essence, there is one key takeaway to get from this lecture note.

\medskip 

Let \(X_1,\ldots,X_n \overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} N(\mu,\sigma^2)\), where \(\mu, \sigma^2\) are both unknown. 
The formula for \textbf{bias-corrected sample variance}, or, \textbf{unbiased sample variance}, which I think is equivalent to the \textbf{bias-corrected MLE}, or, \textbf{unbiased MLE}, is given by 

\begin{equation}\label{eq:unbiased sample var}
    s^2 = \frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{(X_i - \bar{X})}^2,
\end{equation}

where \(\bar{X}\) represents the sample mean \(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{X_i}{n}\). 
With this formula, Geoff provides a pretty comprehensive outline of the derivation of the following key result. 

\begin{theorem}[Distribution of unbiased sample variance]\label{thm:dist of sample var}
    Let \(X_1,\ldots,X_n \overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} N(\mu,\sigma^2)\), where \(\mu, \sigma^2\) are both unknown.
    Then, given the established formula in \cref{eq:unbiased sample var} for \(s^2\), 
    \[\frac{(n-1)s^2}{\sigma^2} \sim \chi_{n-1}^{2}.\]
\end{theorem}

In its most basic form, I prefer to think about \cref{thm:dist of sample var} as follows. 

\begin{theorem}[Distribution of sum of squares]\label{thm:dist of sum of squares}
    With the same conditions as \cref{thm:dist of sample var},
    \[\frac{1}{\sigma^2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{(X_i - \bar{X})}^2 \sim \chi_{n-1}^{2}.\]
\end{theorem}
